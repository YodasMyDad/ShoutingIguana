Shouting Iguana now updates itself. It looks for a new version when it starts, tells you what's new, and installs it when you say so. A crawl is never stopped for an update. Settings › Updates has the check, and a switch to stop the app looking by itself. A copy installed from the .deb package is told when a new version is out, and you download it.

Sites that block crawlers are now read. Browser fallback is on by default: when a site refuses the crawl, it is read in the computer's own Chrome or Edge, out of sight, and most checks let that through. A site that wants a person to tick a box opens a window for you to tick it, and the crawl carries on from there. When a site starts turning the crawl away partway through, the crawl slows down by itself and asks again for the pages it refused. Turn the fallback off in the crawl's settings.

The SEO checks were held to real pages, and 169 of the 170 have now been seen to fire on a real site. What those pages showed is mended: sitemaps written as RSS or Atom are read, a page named only by an hreflang or rel="next" link is checked for how it answers, scripts and stylesheets are judged by their size once unpacked, and a page that declares its charset as "unicode" is read properly.
