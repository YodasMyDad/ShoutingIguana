1. Pages or files that can't be foundInternal URL returns 4xx · Error

9 of 4,521 addresses · takes 2 points off the score · usually fixed by you or your developer

What's wrong, and why it matters

Some addresses on your site answer with an error instead of a page or file, most often "not found" (a 404). Visitors who follow a link to one get an error page, and Google drops addresses like these from its results. That's the right answer for something you removed on purpose, as long as nothing still links to it.

How to fix it

  1. Look at each address in the list and decide whether it should still exist.
  2. If it should, put the page or file back.
  3. If it has moved, forward the old address to the new one (a "301 redirect"), in your site's redirect settings or through your developer.
  4. If it's gone for good, leave it, and remove the links that still point to it.

2. Links that lead to broken pagesLinks to broken internal URLs · Error

5 of 265 pages · takes 4 points off the score · usually fixed by you, in your site's editor

What's wrong, and why it matters

Some of your pages have links that lead to an address on your site that doesn't work. Visitors who click one land on an error page, and search engines waste their visit on it. A link in a menu or footer repeats on every page, so one bad link can show up hundreds of times.

How to fix it

  1. Open each page in the list in your site's editor and find the link.
  2. Point it at the right page, or remove it.
  3. If the same link is on most pages, it's in your menu, footer or page design: fix it there once.

3. Large images that slow pages downLarge images · Warning

61 of 265 pages · takes 3 points off the score · usually fixed by you, in your site's editor

What's wrong, and why it matters

Some pages show images whose files are over 200 KB. Big images are the most common reason a page feels slow, most of all on a phone, and people leave slow pages. Photos uploaded straight from a camera or phone are the usual cause.

How to fix it

  1. Resize each image so it's no bigger than it's shown on the page.
  2. Save it as a compressed JPEG or WebP, then upload it in place of the old one.
  3. If your site has an image-optimising setting or plugin, turn it on so new uploads are shrunk for you.

4. Pages that share the same search descriptionDuplicate meta description · Warning

8 of 265 pages · takes 2 points off the score · usually fixed by you, in your site's editor

What's wrong, and why it matters

Two or more pages share one search description: the short text under a page's title in search results. Searchers can't tell the pages apart, and Google tends to write its own text instead. It's also called a "meta description", and often comes from a default that the site's design fills in.

How to fix it

  1. Open each page in your site's editor and find its description, often under "SEO" or "Search engine listing".
  2. Write one or two sentences that say what's on that page and nowhere else.
  3. Leave numbered pages of one list (page 2, page 3) until last: they matter least.
